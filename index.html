Dashi math

Dashi is five minutes of work and one piece of arithmetic - and yet recipes say "a piece of kombu" and "a handful of flakes" as if pieces and hands came in one size. Every dashi quantity is a percentage of the water in the pot.

Measure the water

The problem it solves

One percent kombu and two and a half percent bonito is the classic ichiban ratio - but the recipe that says so also assumes you know how much water "a pot" holds. Double the servings and the kelp comes up short; halve them and the flakes turn the soup to smoke. Dashi math turns water, servings and percentages into grams: the kombu for the steep, the flakes for the finish, and the whole pot scaled from how many bowls you are filling.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. Kombu varies by harvest, flakes by shave, and the strength bands here are flagged conventions, not measurements of your ingredients. It sizes the pot - you still taste the stock.
